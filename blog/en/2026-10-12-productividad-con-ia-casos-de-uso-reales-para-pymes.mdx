# Productivity with AI: real use cases for SMEs

> Artificial intelligence is no longer the exclusive territory of large corporations. SMEs that know where to apply it gain real hours each week without hiring anyone else.

## The problem: too much work, too few resources

A ten-person SME doesn't have an operations department, a financial controller, or a customer service team on standby. It has the same people doing everything: budgeting, answering emails, managing projects, meeting deadlines and, if there's time left, thinking about how to grow.

In that context, the promise of AI sounds good on paper, but the real question is: where do I use it in my specific business? Not in Amazon's business. In mine, with my clients, my invoices and my three administrative staff members.

The good news is that the most profitable use cases don't require large investments or technical training. They require knowing exactly which repetitive tasks are stealing productive time and replacing them with tools that already exist.

## Use cases where AI is already delivering results

**Writing and professional communication**

Writing sales proposals, answering difficult emails, drafting standard contracts or preparing internal reports consumes hours that few SMEs can afford to waste. A well-configured AI assistant can generate the first draft in seconds. The team reviews, adjusts and sends. What used to take forty minutes is resolved in ten.

The trick is having well-defined context templates: who you are, who you're addressing and what tone to use. With that, the results are usable from the first attempt.

**Document summarization and analysis**

Reviewing a twenty-page contract, extracting key points from an industry report or comparing multiple supplier offers are tasks that can now be delegated to AI. You feed it the document, ask the specific question and get the summary in less than a minute.

For law firms and consultancies, this changes the billable time equation. For SMEs with lots of customer documentation, it reduces mistakes from oversight.

**After-hours customer service**

A chatbot with AI trained on your knowledge base can resolve frequently asked questions, qualify leads and gather basic information when your team isn't available. It doesn't replace personalized service, but it does filter volume and ensures that no inquiry goes without an initial response for hours.

**Automation of administrative tasks**

Sorting emails, reminding about follow-ups, generating draft invoices or detecting contract deadlines are tasks perfect for AI. They don't require sophisticated human judgment, but they do consume time if you do them manually. Automating them frees people up for work that actually requires their presence.

## What really holds SMEs back

It's not the cost. Most useful tools cost less than thirty euros a month. What holds them back is fragmentation: one tool for invoices, another for contracts, another for tasks, another for team chat. When AI lives in a separate silo, it lacks context and its usefulness is cut in half.

## How to implement it without wasting time in the process

These steps work in real SMEs, not in Harvard case studies:

- **Choose a single task to start.** The one that consumes the most time and brings the least strategic value. Master it with AI before adding more.
- **Train the tool with your context.** A generic prompt gives generic results. Describe your business, your industry and your tone once well, and reuse it always.
- **Measure the time saved.** Without a metric, you don't know if it works. Time it before and after. If there's no real improvement, change your approach.
- **Integrate where you already work.** AI that lives within your daily workflow gets used. The kind that requires opening another tab doesn't.
- **Don't automate what requires judgment.** AI is effective at repetitive and predictable tasks. Business decisions, relationships with key clients and strategy are still yours.
