# How to transform team meetings into real decisions

> Too many meetings end without anyone knowing what they have to do or when. This has a solution, and it doesn't involve meeting less, but meeting better.

## The problem isn't the meeting, it's what happens before and after

Spain is one of the European countries where the most hours are spent in meetings. And yet, the most common feeling when leaving them is that "it could have been an email". The problem is rarely the meeting itself: it's the lack of structure before entering and the absence of follow-up when leaving.

A meeting without clear decisions is, at best, wasted time. At worst, it generates confusion: each person walks away with a different interpretation of what was agreed, nobody takes responsibility for anything, and the topic reappears in the next meeting, as if the previous one had never happened.

The antidote is not to eliminate meetings. It's to turn them into engines of action. For that, you need to work on three moments: preparation, development, and closure with follow-up.

## Before the meeting: define the purpose with precision

Most meetings fail before they start. They are called without a clear objective, with too many people and without prior information. The result is that the first twenty minutes are lost putting everyone in context.

**Define what type of meeting it is.** A meeting to make a decision is not the same as one to share information or solve a problem. Each format requires a different dynamic. Mixing them in the same call is one of the most frequent causes of chaos.

**Send the agenda in advance.** Not a list of topics, but specific questions that need answers: *Do we approve the proposed budget? Which alternative do we choose for client X?* When participants know what will be decided, they arrive with an informed opinion, not to improvise.

**Invite only those who need to be there.** Each additional person multiplies coordination time and dilutes responsibility. If someone only needs to be informed of the outcome, send them a summary afterwards.

## During the meeting: facilitate, don't monopolize

A well-facilitated meeting moves forward. A meeting without a facilitator drifts.

**Someone has to be at the helm.** Not to talk more, but to keep the focus: remind everyone of the objective when the conversation drifts, give the floor to those who haven't spoken, and above all, reformulate the agreements out loud before moving to the next point. *"So, we agreed that Juan will review the proposal before Friday. Correct?"*

**Document in real time.** You don't need a formal record or written paragraphs. Just three columns: decision made, responsible person, deadline. If no one takes these notes during the meeting, it's very likely that no one will remember them well the next day.

**Manage time rigorously.** If there are four points on the agenda and forty-five minutes, assign time to each one. When a topic runs long, consciously decide whether it deserves more time or if a specific meeting should be scheduled to resolve it. What you cannot do is leave the last points unaddressed due to lack of time.

## After the meeting: the follow-up that changes everything

This is where most teams fail. The meeting ends, everyone goes back to their work, and the agreements evaporate.

These are the steps that turn a meeting into concrete results:

- **Send the summary in less than two hours.** Not an extensive record. Three or four lines with the decisions made, who is responsible for each one, and when it needs to be ready. Brief and direct.
- **Create the tasks immediately.** If the agreements are not translated into assigned tasks with dates, they remain in limbo. Tools like MITIKUS task management tools allow you to create them directly from the meeting context, without intermediate steps.
- **Review the status in the next meeting.** The first five minutes of any recurring meeting should be spent reviewing what was agreed on last time and what has been done. This generates real accountability and eliminates the syndrome of the endless meeting.
- **Close open topics before opening new ones.** It's tempting to always talk about what's new and urgent. But if previous agreements are not fulfilled, no future meeting will be of any use.
