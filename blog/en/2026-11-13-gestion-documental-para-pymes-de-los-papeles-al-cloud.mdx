# Document management for SMEs: from papers to the cloud

> Lost invoices, unsigned contracts and filing cabinets taking up half the conference room. If this sounds familiar, it's time to make the jump to the cloud once and for all.

## The documentary chaos that holds back SMEs

There comes a moment in any SME's life when the volume of papers becomes unmanageable. A tax inspection arrives and it takes two hours to locate an invoice from three years ago. A client asks for a copy of the contract they signed and no one remembers which folder it's in. An employee goes on sick leave and their onboarding documents disappear with them.

This chaos is not an organizational problem. It's a system problem. Or rather, not having one at all.

The good news is that digitizing document management for an SME doesn't require an IT team or an exorbitant investment. It requires judgment, the right tools and the willingness to change a habit that, in many cases, has been around for years.

## Why paper is still the silent enemy

Paper has a real cost that few companies calculate. According to estimates from consultancies specializing in business processes, a worker spends between 30 and 40 minutes a day searching for information or documents. In a ten-person SME, that amounts to over 300 hours lost per month.

But beyond time, the problem is fragility. A paper document can be lost, deteriorate, be filed incorrectly or simply not be available when someone needs it outside the office. In an environment where remote or mobile work is already the norm, relying on paper is a competitive drag.

Add to this regulatory compliance. The GDPR requires protecting customer and employee information. A filing cabinet with personal data accessible to anyone passing through the room doesn't meet those requirements. Digital document management, with controlled access permissions, does.

## How to make the transition without dying trying

The most common mistake when digitizing documents is trying to do it all at once. You scan for a week, upload everything to a folder called "miscellaneous documents" and six months later the situation is just as chaotic, only in digital format.

The transition works when approached in layers:

**1. Define the structure first, then migrate content**
Before scanning a single piece of paper, design the folder tree or categories you're going to use. By client? By document type? By year? There's no universal answer, but there is a rule: anyone on the team should be able to find any document in less than two minutes.

**2. Set a clear cutoff point**
You don't need to digitize the entire company history all at once. Decide from what date you start working digitally and keep the old physical archive as is, well labeled. Over time, you can gradually digitize the historical records in blocks.

**3. Automate new documents from the start**
The invoices you generate, the contracts you sign, the quotes you send: all of these can be born digital. There's no point printing them to file them. If you use a tool that allows you to create, send and store these documents in a single workflow, you eliminate paper from the root.

**4. Control who accesses what**
One of the great benefits of the cloud is permissions management. Not all employees need access to all contracts or supplier invoices. Configuring appropriate access levels protects information and ensures compliance.

## The keys to a document system that works long-term

Once the workflow is digitized, the challenge is to maintain it. These are the principles that distinguish SMEs that succeed:

- **Coherent naming:** all files follow the same name format. Something as simple as `2026-11_Invoice_ClientX_001.pdf` avoids duplicates and endless searches.
- **Periodic review:** each quarter, review whether there are documents that can be deleted or permanently archived. A clean system is a usable system.
- **Integrated digital signature:** contracts sent to customers and suppliers should be able to be signed without printing them. Digital signatures have full legal validity in Spain and greatly streamline closings.
