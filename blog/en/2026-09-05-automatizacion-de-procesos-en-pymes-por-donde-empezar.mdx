# Process automation in SMEs: where to start

> Automation is not just for large companies with million-dollar budgets. It's a strategic decision that any SME can make today, if you know where to start.

## The real problem: time slipping away without anyone controlling it

In most SMEs, the biggest brake on growth is not a lack of customers or market issues. It's time. Time spent on repetitive tasks, searching for documents, sending manual reminders, reconciling spreadsheets that a system should complete.

The human team ends up trapped in operations, and strategy is always left for tomorrow. This is the clearest symptom that processes need review.

The good news is that automation doesn't require a radical technological transformation. In most cases, it's enough to identify three or four tasks that repeat every week and find a way for them to happen on their own, or with much less manual effort.

## Step 1: Map before you automate

The most common mistake is diving into implementing tools without understanding what you want to improve. Before touching any software, it's worth doing a simple exercise: for a week, write down everything you and your team do manually and repetitively.

Some questions that help identify clear automation candidates:

- What tasks do you perform exactly the same way each time, with barely any variation?
- What information do you copy from one place to another (emails, spreadsheets, forms)?
- What processes depend on someone remembering to do them?
- Where do most errors or delays occur?

With that inventory in hand, prioritize by impact. Don't start with the most complex. Start with what steals the most time or generates the most errors.

## Step 2: Choose processes with the highest immediate return

There are areas where automation has a quick and visible impact in any SME:

**Invoicing and collections.** Generating invoices manually, sending them, tracking unpaid invoices… is a process that can be drastically reduced. Automating recurring invoice sending and payment reminders frees up hours each month and reduces bad debt.

**Document management.** Contracts, quotes, delivery notes: documents that are created, sent, signed, and filed. If each step requires manual intervention, the process is slow and error-prone. With well-defined workflows, each document follows its path without anyone having to push it.

**Time tracking and attendance.** Hour registration is still a manual process in many SMEs, with all that entails: errors, discrepancies, time spent reconciling it at month-end. Automating it is straightforward and the return is immediate.

**Customer follow-up.** Remembering to call a customer, send a follow-up proposal, or notify contract expiration are tasks that should happen on their own, triggered by conditions or dates, not by someone's memory.

## Step 3: Implement progressively and measure

Automating everything at once is a guarantee of chaos. The key is to go in phases:

1. **Automate one process, measure the impact, and consolidate.** How much time has been saved? Have errors gone down? Does the team use it naturally?
2. **Look for tools that centralize, not multiply.** Adding a new tool for each process creates information silos and more complexity. Ideally, you want a platform that covers multiple needs from one place.
3. **Train your team before activating.** Automation fails when people don't understand why it exists or how it works. A brief onboarding session makes the difference between real adoption and silent rejection.
4. **Document the automated process.** If the workflow exists only in the head of whoever configured it, it's as fragile as the manual process it replaced.

One final note: don't confuse automation with elimination of human judgment. Routine processes are automated; complex decisions are not. The goal is for your team to dedicate their energy to what truly requires judgment, creativity, or relationship.
