# Automate your payment reminders and collect sooner

> Chasing unpaid invoices consumes time and energy you need for your business. With automatic reminders, money arrives faster and you stop acting as a debt collector.

## The silent problem draining your cash flow

You finish a project, send the invoice and... wait. The due dates pass and payment doesn't arrive. You send a careful email, almost embarrassed, reminding them they owe you money. The client apologizes, says they forgot, and promises to pay "this week". This happens to most freelancers and SMEs in Spain, and the cost isn't just financial: it's the time you waste managing follow-ups and the tension it creates in your client relationship.

According to data from the Morality Observatory, the average payment period in Spain frequently exceeds 60 days, well above the legal limit of 30 days for commercial operations. The most common cause isn't client bad faith, but simple forgetfulness. Invoices pile up, administration departments have their own pace and, if nobody reminds them, payment gets delayed.

The good news is that this problem has a direct solution: automate payment reminders before, during and after the due date. You don't need more bureaucracy; you need a system that works for you while you focus on what really matters.

## What an effective reminder sequence should include

A well-designed payment reminder isn't an aggressive late payment notice. It's useful communication that helps the client meet their obligation without friction. The key is in the sequence: when it's sent, what tone it uses and what information it includes.

**Reminder before due date (3-5 days before)**
This message serves a preventive function. The client receives the invoice, sees it's due soon and prioritizes it. The tone is neutral and informative: "We remind you that invoice X is due on day Y. Here's the link to view it."

**Reminder on the due date**
A timely notice, without drama. Many clients need this push to move things through their internal processes. Always include the amount, invoice reference and preferred payment method to eliminate any excuse like "I don't know where to send the transfer".

**First late payment notice (3-7 days after due date)**
The tone shifts slightly here. It remains professional, but clearly indicates that payment is overdue and requests confirmation of the expected payment date. Attaching a copy of the invoice reduces response time.

**Second late payment notice (15-20 days after due date)**
At this point it's worth mentioning, without threatening, that prolonged non-payment may result in service suspension or surcharges as agreed in the contract. This reminder usually speeds up resolution.

## How to set up automation without technical complications

Automation doesn't mean programming. Most modern management tools allow you to define these sequences in just a few minutes, linked directly to the invoices you issue.

The basic steps are:

1. **Define your payment terms** when creating each invoice: 15, 30 or 60 days depending on your agreement with the client.
2. **Set up your reminder sequence** with the days before or after the due date that you choose for each message.
3. **Personalize the templates** with the client's name, amount, reference and payment link. A personalized message has a much higher response rate than a generic one.
4. **Activate automatic sending** and let the system manage follow-up without your intervention.

With MITIKUS, for example, you can link this automation directly to your invoicing module: as soon as you issue an invoice, the reminder sequence is programmed without any additional steps.
