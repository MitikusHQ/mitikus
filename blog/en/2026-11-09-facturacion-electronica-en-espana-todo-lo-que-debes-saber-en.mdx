# Electronic invoicing in Spain: everything you need to know in 2026

> The mandatory requirement for electronic invoicing between companies is already a reality in Spain. If you haven't yet adapted your business, this article explains what the law requires, who it affects, and how to comply without complications.

## The change you can no longer ignore

For years, paper invoices or PDFs sent by email were sufficient for any freelancer or SME. That has changed. The Growth and Creation Law, developed through the Electronic Invoicing Regulation, establishes the obligation to issue and receive invoices in structured electronic format in relationships between companies and professionals (B2B).

The legislator's objective is twofold: reduce late payments and improve tax control. For the business owner, this means adapting your processes before sanctions knock on your door.

What many businesses still don't understand is that sending a PDF is not enough. The regulation requires structured formats—such as Facturae or UBL—that machines can read and interpret automatically. A PDF, however well-formatted, does not meet that requirement.

## Who does it affect and since when?

Implementation is being carried out in stages according to annual billing volume:

- **Companies with billing over 8 million euros:** obligation already in force since 2025.
- **Other freelancers and SMEs:** the obligation applies progressively throughout 2026, according to the final schedule published by the Ministry of Economics.

If you're unsure which bracket you fall into, check your last Corporate Income Tax return or your self-employed income tax return. Your previous year's turnover is what determines your deadline.

Important: the obligation affects **B2B** relationships (company to company or professional to company). Invoices to end consumers (B2C) are excluded from this framework for now, although they remain subject to general invoicing rules.

**What about transactions with the Public Administration?** Those have been electronic for years through the FACe system. What's new is the extension to the private sector.

## What you need to comply with the regulation

Adapting is not as complex as it seems, but it requires making concrete decisions:

**1. Choose the correct format**
The accepted formats are Facturae (the Spanish standard), UBL 2.1, and CII (Cross Industry Invoice). Your accountant or invoicing software must be able to generate one of them.

**2. Use an accredited platform or your own solution**
You can opt for a private exchange platform, as long as it meets the technical requirements of the regulation, or for solutions integrated into your own management system. The key is that the recipient can also receive and process the electronic invoice, so interoperability between platforms is essential.

**3. Properly retain electronic invoices**
The retention obligation is four years for commercial purposes and up to six for tax purposes. Invoices must be saved in their original format, guaranteeing their integrity and authenticity. Simply downloading the XML and deleting it from the system won't work.

**4. Acknowledge receipt**
One of the most relevant innovations: the recipient has the obligation to confirm receipt of the invoice within a specified period. This changes the usual dynamics and requires that both issuer and recipient have systems that manage these statuses (received, accepted, rejected, paid).

## Practical tips for the transition

These are the steps we recommend for making the change wisely:

- **Audit your volume of B2B invoices.** Knowing how many invoices you issue and receive monthly helps you assess the effort required.
